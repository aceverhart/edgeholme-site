What Size Outdoor Wall Sconce Should Go on the Sides of Your Front Door?

The most common mistake in exterior lighting is choosing a fixture that's too small. Here's how to size wall sconces correctly — for front doors, garage doors, and everything in between.

Walk through almost any new subdivision and you'll see the same problem repeated on every third house: a pair of small lanterns flanking a front door on a two-story facade. The fixtures look fine in the product photo. On the actual house, they disappear. The entry looks unfinished. The lighting looks like an afterthought.

Sizing outdoor wall sconces is one of those things that's easy to get wrong because most people are buying fixtures in a store or online — divorced from the actual scale of the wall they're going on. This guide gives you a practical framework so you don't have to guess.

The Basic Rule: Fixture Height Should Be One-Quarter to One-Third of Door Height

For a standard 8-foot (96-inch) entry door, that puts your target fixture height between 24 and 32 inches. For a 9-foot door, you're looking at 27 to 36 inches. For a 10-foot door, 30 to 40 inches.

This rule isn't arbitrary — it's proportional. The fixture needs to read at the scale of the door, not disappear next to it. A 12-inch lantern on an 8-foot door is like putting a small picture frame on a 10-foot wall. Technically present, visually lost.

Quick Reference

Fixture height = ¼ to ⅓ of door height. For an 8-foot door, that's 24–32 inches. When in doubt, go with the larger end of the range — you'll never wish the fixture were smaller once it's on the wall.

Sizing by Application

Door height is the starting point, but it's not the only variable. Here's how to think about sizing across the most common exterior applications.

Application Recommended Size Notes
Standard 8-foot entry door 24" – 30" Pair flanking the door, centered 8–12" from casing
Tall 9–10 foot entry door 30" – 36" Taller door demands more fixture height to stay proportional
Single-car garage door 24" – 36" One fixture centered on each side panel, not on the door itself
Double-car garage door 36" – 48" The wider the opening, the taller the fixture needs to be to anchor it
Tall facade (12+ feet) 36" – 48" Scale to wall height, not just door height
Covered porch column 24" – 36" Consider column width — fixture should be narrower than the column face

Garage Doors Deserve More Attention Than They Get

On most American homes, the garage door is the largest single element of the facade. It takes up more visual real estate than the front door, the windows, and sometimes the entire first floor combined. And it usually gets the worst lighting.

The instinct is to put a single small fixture above the garage door, centered. This is almost always wrong for two reasons. First, a centered overhead fixture creates flat, unflattering light that washes out the entire surface. Second, a single small fixture over a 16-foot-wide door is visually weightless — it reads as decorative at best, absent at worst.

The better approach: a pair of vertical wall sconces, one on each side of the garage door, mounted on the pilasters or wall panels flanking the opening. This anchors the corners of the door, creates depth and shadow that makes the facade more three-dimensional, and scales appropriately to the opening width.

For a standard double-car garage door (roughly 16 feet wide), fixtures in the 36–48 inch range work well. For a single-car door (8–9 feet), 24–36 inches is appropriate.

Designer Note

If your garage and front entry use different fixture sizes — which is common when proportions differ — make sure they're from the same family. Mixing fixture silhouettes across the same facade is visually noisy. Same profile, different scale, is how you maintain coherence.

Where to Mount Them

For entry door applications, the standard mounting height is 66–72 inches to the center of the fixture — roughly eye level for most adults. This puts the light source at a useful height and keeps the fixture in the visual field of someone approaching the door.

For garage door applications, mounting height depends more on the architecture. The fixture should sit at a height that visually relates to the top of the door opening — typically 84–96 inches to center for a standard 7-foot garage door.

The most common mistake is mounting fixtures too high. Higher feels more dramatic in theory; in practice, it pushes the light above where it's useful and makes the fixture look like it's trying to escape the door it's supposed to be flanking.

Don't Forget Width

Fixture height gets all the attention, but width matters too — especially for vertical bar sconces like the Arden. The fixture should be narrow enough that it reads as a vertical element, not a block. For most residential applications, a 3–5 inch wide fixture works well. Wider than that and the silhouette starts to feel heavy against most door casings.

The Arden is 4 inches wide across all three sizes — proportioned to stay slim regardless of which height you choose.

The Short Version

Size your fixture to one-quarter to one-third of door height. Go taller rather than shorter when you're on the edge. Pair fixtures on either side of garage doors rather than centering above them. Keep fixture width under 5 inches for most residential applications. And if everything else is even — choose the larger size. Nobody has ever stood in front of a finished exterior and wished the lights were smaller.
